# Improving the Cone Display

We should test the updated Mac app with our TV and cone before buying hardware. We want a clear live image that people can see while walking around it. A fan or projector could help, but neither automatically creates a true 360 degree view.

## What we can improve now

The TV creates the image, and the cone reflects it. The new app preserves more camera detail, keeps proportions correct, and removes software background glow. Four, six, or eight repeated views may improve coverage, but more copies can create narrower images, duplicates, and gaps.

One camera cannot capture the hidden sides or back of a person. Repeating its image does not change that. The original Pepper's Cone research used calibration to correct distortion; our setup needs its own measurements [1].

## A fan in the center

An LED display fan could create a bright, floating-looking image. Its LEDs sweep across a flat disc, so spinning does not create a complete three dimensional person. Some models accept live input; we must check the exact device [2].

Inside the cone, a fan could add unwanted reflections and block TV light. Test a protected fan separately first. The cone is not a blade guard. Follow the manufacturer's mounting, clearance, and protection requirements [2].

## Using a projector

A normal projector needs an image-forming surface. Projecting directly onto the transparent cone is not a reliable route to all-angle visibility. We could instead project onto a screen beneath it, replacing the TV. The cone's viewing limits would remain.

Researchers have demonstrated different views around a display using a high speed projector, synchronized rotating optics, and a special diffuser [3]. That would be a separate hardware project.

## Our next step

Test twelve positions around the cone, including transitions between views. Check faces, orientation, duplicates, and motion, then try two viewers on opposite sides. Push only after the reflected image is clearly better, leaving Windows unchanged. If coverage is still poor, measure and calibrate the cone before adding hardware.
